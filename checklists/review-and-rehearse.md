# Review and rehearsal checks

Record PASS, FAIL, or NOT CHECKED beside each item, with a date, responsible role, and evidence location. A missing observation is NOT CHECKED, not PASS. These are proposed working criteria, not a certified quality standard. Adapt the support method with the people who need it.

## Before rehearsal

| Check | PASS observation | FAIL or NOT CHECKED response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source context | The reviewer can locate the current sentence or passage for each selected term. | Obtain the current source; reopen entries from an older outline. |
| Meaning | The speaker has explained ambiguous intended meanings. | Mark the term BLOCKED; do not let a word list decide the theology. |
| Review | The selected language and variety are confirmed, and a competent language reviewer has checked proposed wording in the complete sentence. | Keep the entry PENDING; do not label a machine suggestion approved. |
| Names and references | Names match the speaker's checked source; book, chapter, and verse numbers match the intended passage. | Resolve differences with the speaker and record the correction. |
| Available support | The named people, materials, devices, and correction channel are available for this service. | State the actual gap and choose a support method that can be provided. |

## During rehearsal

Use a short sample from the planned sermon containing the selected terms, an announcement, a negation or condition, and a reference where those occur naturally. This is coverage of known risks, not a statistical accuracy test. Do not insert artificial statements into the actual sermon merely to exercise the checklist.

| Check | PASS observation | FAIL or NOT CHECKED response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Audio | A reviewer can understand the sample from the actual input used by the system or interpreter. | Correct source level, microphone placement, or competing sound and replay. If audio is unavailable, the input check is NOT CHECKED. |
| Transcript, when exposed | A reviewer compares the actual speech with the source transcript; the selected names, numbers, conditions, and claims are preserved. | Log `INPUT_OR_ASR`; keep the translation diagnosis open until the transcript is checked. |
| Target meaning | A competent language reviewer compares the received target output with the intended message in context. | Log `TRANSLATION` only when the source transcript is known to be correct; otherwise use `UNKNOWN` or `MULTIPLE`. |
| Attribution | A quotation, paraphrase, example, or speaker change remains identifiable. | Record the confusing segment and agree on a clearer cue with the speaker. |
| Use on the receiving side | A person who uses the format can follow the sample, locate corrections, and describe any gaps. | Change the format or support plan; do not infer usability from the operator's screen. |
| Recovery | The team demonstrates the agreed correction/fallback action on the actual receiving side. | Practice it or select an available alternative. An invented fallback is a failure. |

## Decide and record

Treat a changed negation, referent, named person, passage reference, or practical instruction as a meaning failure even when the rest of the sample looks fluent. A cosmetic punctuation issue may be minor only if the reviewer confirms that meaning and readability remain usable.

For each failure write: observed evidence → who decides → correction or fallback → what recipients are told → recheck evidence. If a reliable correction cannot reach recipients, stop describing the affected output as reliable and use the agreed alternative. Do not silently rewrite a speaker's theological claim.

The local service owner can mark the selected sample ready only after the required checks are observed and unresolved meaning failures have a demonstrated resolution. This is a bounded rehearsal result. It does not establish accuracy for untested language pairs, unscripted sections, another room, another device, or a later service.

## After a change or service

- Recheck the affected sentences when an outline or term choice changes.
- Keep the previous decision and the reason for the revision in the private issue log.
- Share the corrected version with the people who will actually use it.
- Retain only material the team has permission and a reason to keep. Remove identifying or sensitive content before sharing a reusable example publicly.
