01 / START HERE

A plan your team can share

  1. Choose a short, current source sample.Ask the speaker for the surrounding sentences. Select names, references, recurring terms and places where a changed word could change the meaning.
  2. Confirm the language and variety.Use one register for each source and target language pair. Identify a competent language reviewer; a machine suggestion is not an approval.
  3. Record context before choosing wording.Put the sentence, intended sense and evidence location into the register. Ask the speaker to resolve ambiguous intended meanings.
  4. Make each decision visible.Use PENDING or BLOCKED when a choice is unresolved. Record the reviewer’s role, date, approved wording and reason when a decision is ready.
  5. Rehearse on the receiving side.Use the actual support method. Check names, negations, references and attribution; ask a person who uses the format to try the correction route.
  6. Log and reopen changes.Keep a dated private copy. Recheck affected entries when wording, language variety, devices or support arrangements change.

A word list cannot decide meaning on its own. These are proposed working materials, not a certified quality standard. Keep private sermons, people’s names and pastoral information out of public examples.

03 / REVIEW PROMPTS

Ask what the sentence means

Open a term or pattern for its review question, evidence to record and response if the question remains unresolved.

gracecontext-dependent term

What meaning does the speaker intend in this sentence?

Evidence to record: Speaker explains intended use; language reviewer checks the complete sentence.

If unresolved: Hold wording until the intended use is clear.

faithcontext-dependent term

Does the surrounding argument change how the word should be understood?

Evidence to record: Reviewer records the meaning in context and the reason for the chosen wording.

If unresolved: Ask for the surrounding sentence and reopen the entry.

salvationcontext-dependent term

What explanation and audience knowledge does this sermon assume?

Evidence to record: Speaker supplies context; reviewer checks how the audience will understand the wording.

If unresolved: Ask the speaker to explain rather than insert an unsupported definition.

covenantcontext-dependent term

Is the speaker naming a particular passage or using the term more broadly?

Evidence to record: Reference or intended use is recorded and reviewed.

If unresolved: Block an unexplained word-only substitution.

communioncontext-dependent term

Is this a named part of the service or another use of the word?

Evidence to record: Speaker and language reviewer agree on the intended use for this sentence.

If unresolved: Resolve the intended use before approving wording.

baptismcontext-dependent term

Does the wording rely on a local practice that the listener may not know?

Evidence to record: Relevant explanation from the speaker and the reviewer's context check are recorded.

If unresolved: Ask for the missing explanation without choosing doctrine for the church.

kingdomcontext-dependent term

Which phrase and referent surround this word?

Evidence to record: Complete phrase and intended referent are reviewed together.

If unresolved: Reopen wording if the referent is unclear.

spiritcontext-dependent term

What does the word refer to in this occurrence?

Evidence to record: Referent and surrounding sentence are supplied and reviewed.

If unresolved: Do not infer meaning from capitalization alone.

fleshcontext-dependent term

Does the speaker mean a physical description or use an expression requiring explanation?

Evidence to record: Speaker's intended sense and the reviewer's reasoning are recorded.

If unresolved: Ask about the sentence instead of assuming a technical definition.

churchcontext-dependent term

Does this mean a building or a group or another stated referent?

Evidence to record: Referent is clear in both the source context and reviewed wording.

If unresolved: Ask the speaker to identify the referent.

elderrole or title

Is this a named role or a description of age in this sentence?

Evidence to record: Local use and intended role are recorded before review.

If unresolved: Do not assign a role from the isolated word.

personal and place namesproper noun

Which spelling and spoken pronunciation identify the actual person or place?

Evidence to record: Authorized source or speaker confirms the name; the rehearsal preserves the referent.

If unresolved: Correct the record and replay the relevant phrase.

book chapter and versereference

Do the spoken reference and displayed reference identify the same passage?

Evidence to record: Reviewer compares book name and each number against the speaker's chosen reference.

If unresolved: Resolve the mismatch before using the displayed reference.

not never unlesslogical relation

Does the output preserve the condition or negation?

Evidence to record: Reviewer compares the complete proposition including the qualifying word.

If unresolved: Treat changed meaning as a stop-and-correct issue.

numbers times and datespractical instruction

Could a changed value send someone to the wrong time or place?

Evidence to record: The announced value matches the checked service information.

If unresolved: Correct through an available audience channel and recheck.

idiom or wordplayexpression

Would literal wording preserve what the speaker intends?

Evidence to record: Speaker explains the point; language reviewer checks a comprehensible rendering.

If unresolved: Replace or explain the source expression with the speaker's agreement.

quotation or paraphraseattribution

Is the speaker quoting a specific text or giving a paraphrase?

Evidence to record: Source and version are recorded; the presentation preserves the distinction.

If unresolved: Do not silently substitute a familiar quotation for spoken words.

we they this itreference

Can a listener identify the intended person or thing from the surrounding material?

Evidence to record: Reviewer identifies the same referent from the output and its context.

If unresolved: Ask the speaker to name the referent or restore the missing context.

04 / REHEARSAL

Observe it before marking PASS

For each check, record PASS, FAIL or NOT CHECKED with a date, responsible role and evidence location. A missing observation is NOT CHECKED.

Before rehearsal

Source context

PASS observation: The reviewer can locate the current sentence or passage for each selected term.

FAIL or NOT CHECKED: Obtain the current source; reopen entries from an older outline.

Meaning

PASS observation: The speaker has explained ambiguous intended meanings.

FAIL or NOT CHECKED: Mark the term BLOCKED; do not let a word list decide the theology.

Review

PASS observation: The selected language and variety are confirmed, and a competent language reviewer has checked proposed wording in the complete sentence.

FAIL or NOT CHECKED: Keep the entry PENDING; do not label a machine suggestion approved.

Names and references

PASS observation: Names match the speaker's checked source; book, chapter, and verse numbers match the intended passage.

FAIL or NOT CHECKED: Resolve differences with the speaker and record the correction.

Available support

PASS observation: The named people, materials, devices, and correction channel are available for this service.

FAIL or NOT CHECKED: State the actual gap and choose a support method that can be provided.

During rehearsal

Audio

PASS observation: A reviewer can understand the sample from the actual input used by the system or interpreter.

FAIL or NOT CHECKED: Correct source level, microphone placement, or competing sound and replay. If audio is unavailable, the input check is NOT CHECKED.

Transcript, when exposed

PASS observation: A reviewer compares the actual speech with the source transcript; the selected names, numbers, conditions, and claims are preserved.

FAIL or NOT CHECKED: Log INPUT_OR_ASR; keep the translation diagnosis open until the transcript is checked.

Target meaning

PASS observation: A competent language reviewer compares the received target output with the intended message in context.

FAIL or NOT CHECKED: Log TRANSLATION only when the source transcript is known to be correct; otherwise use UNKNOWN or MULTIPLE.

Attribution

PASS observation: A quotation, paraphrase, example, or speaker change remains identifiable.

FAIL or NOT CHECKED: Record the confusing segment and agree on a clearer cue with the speaker.

Use on the receiving side

PASS observation: A person who uses the format can follow the sample, locate corrections, and describe any gaps.

FAIL or NOT CHECKED: Change the format or support plan; do not infer usability from the operator's screen.

Recovery

PASS observation: The team demonstrates the agreed correction/fallback action on the actual receiving side.

FAIL or NOT CHECKED: Practice it or select an available alternative. An invented fallback is a failure.

Decide, correct and recheck

Treat a changed negation, referent, named person, passage reference or practical instruction as a meaning failure even when the rest sounds fluent. For each failure, record: evidence → who decides → correction or fallback → what recipients are told → recheck evidence.

The service owner can mark the selected sample ready only after required checks are observed and unresolved meaning failures have a demonstrated resolution. That bounded rehearsal result does not establish accuracy for untested language pairs, unscripted sections or another service.

05 / PRACTICE

Trace the failure before naming the cause

These are fictional, English-only diagnostic exercises. Compare what was spoken with the source transcript first, then decide what the evidence supports. They do not demonstrate translation quality.

Practice C01

Spoken
The meeting is not on Friday.
Source transcript
The meeting is on Friday.
Target output
Not available in this case.
Reveal the diagnosis and recheck

Negation lost before translation

The source transcript removes not and reverses the announcement.

Next action: Check intelligible source audio and correct the transcript or use the available correction channel.

Recheck: The reviewed transcript and the recipient's corrected information both retain the negative statement.

Next action: Check the announced time with the service team and replay the phrase using the actual input.

Recheck: The transcript and corrected receiving-side message identify six fifteen.

06 / OPTIONAL FILE CHECKER

Check the records, not the theology

The worksheets work without code. With Python 3, download and extract the whole kit, open a terminal in its folder, and run:

python validate.py
python validate.py --terms examples/unreviewed-register.csv

Both should pass file checks. The example stays unapproved. To see an incomplete register rejected by the administrative gate, run:

python validate.py --terms templates/term-register.csv --gate

A failure is expected for that blank template. For your own local register, replace the path with your file. The gate requires nonempty approved records with review evidence. A pass confirms required administrative fields; it cannot judge linguistic accuracy, reviewer competence, doctrinal agreement or accessibility.

07 / CONTEXT AND SOURCES

Keep human review in the workflow

Human interpreting, written translation, transcription and captions do different jobs. In a speech-to-text-to-translation pipeline, an error can enter before translation. A glossary or vocabulary hint is not evidence that the final sentence is right.

  • W3C: captions and accuracy review
  • Deepgram: transcription keyterms
  • DeepL: glossary guidance
